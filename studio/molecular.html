How much can two substrate concentrations tell you about an enzyme? Choose a second measurement, compare it with a trained policy, and see which family of kinetic curves the two rates support.

Choose the next measurement

All simulated assays start at 0.11 ppm. The policy groups the initial rate into a bin and estimates the chance of a correct family prediction for each remaining dose. Take one more measurement to submit the episode.

Compare enzyme activity in 3D

Reveal the second rate to unlock playback. Drag to orbit or use the arrow keys. Both scenes use the same scale, so faster turnover is visible as more frequent product release.

Green lobes depict a schematic enzyme, amber depicts substrate, and violet depicts product. Binding and release are staged illustrations. Playback is scaled by the Michaelis–Menten rate. The supervised models use six measured-in-simulation rates per example, while the policy chooses a second dose using the first rate only. Changing the scene’s rate model leaves the policy and its reward unchanged.

Evaluate the trained models

The random forest and two-hidden-layer PyTorch network estimate Vmax and Km from all six rates. The measurement policy gets two rates and predicts the generating family using a fitted Gaussian decoder. Both tasks use the same held-out assays.

Reproduce the experiment

Simulate rates with v = Vmax × S / (Km + S) and additive Gaussian noise. The terminal reward is one for a correct family prediction and zero otherwise. Training uses epsilon-greedy exploration and sample-average Q updates. The labels describe synthetic parameter families. Experimental validation would require independent assay runs. Km is an apparent kinetic parameter and can depend on the reaction mechanism.
