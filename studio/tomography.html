From AI prediction to a testable image

The AI contribution is the published AlphaFold prediction of a single protein chain. Each vertex in the green trace represents one amino acid at its alpha carbon. Gold segments cross the selected slab. The camera rotates independently of the sampling plane.

For each slice we spread each alpha carbon into a Gaussian with a 2.5 Å standard deviation and divide the map by its peak. Brighter pixels indicate more nearby backbone points. The simulated detector sums this proxy along parallel lines. An iterative numerical solver uses those sums to estimate the slice. RMSE compares its output to the known input on the same grid. This exercise measures recovery of the predicted shape. Testing biological accuracy would require independent experimental data.

Try the missing-angle experiment

Keep the slice and pass count fixed. Compare 4 angles with 48, then restrict the scan to 90°. Inspect the small structures and record which change affects density error most. The detector records sums along parallel rays. Each solver pass projects its current estimate, compares those sums with the readings and distributes the residuals back to the pixels.

These noise-free, dimensionless measurements come from the same discretized operator used for reconstruction. This is a controlled inverse problem with known ground truth.
