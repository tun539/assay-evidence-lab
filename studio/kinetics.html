Assumptions, Identifiability & Molecular ML Benchmark Framing

1. Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Concentration Splits

Inspired by activity-cliff and OOD evaluation frameworks from molML (TU Eindhoven), this workbench separates interior interpolation folds from boundary extrapolation folds (lowest and highest [S]). Holding out every replicate at a concentration tests whether a rate law generalizes across saturation regimes rather than memorizing replicate averages.

2. Apparent Parameters, Cooperativity & Substrate Inhibition

Fitting v = Vmax·[S]/(Km + [S]), Cooperative Hill (n), or Haldane substrate inhibition (v = Vmax·[S]/(Km + [S] + [S]²/Ki), peaking at [S]opt = √(Km·Ki)) estimates apparent steady-state parameters. Confirming dead-end ESS complexes, microscopic Kd, or batch-independent Vmax shifts requires orthogonal binding titrations and independent biological preparations.

3. Why Nonlinear Fitting Beats Linearization

Double-reciprocal Lineweaver–Burk plots remain valuable visual diagnostics for distinguishing competitive (Km) from non-competitive (Vmax) shifts, yet unweighted linear regression on 1/v magnifies low-[S] experimental noise as 1/v². All parameters here are estimated by exact positive-parameter nonlinear least squares directly on the measured velocity scale.
